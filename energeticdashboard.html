The GuideLine podcast: 3 posts from 1 series. Launch post: 148 likes, 32 reposts.
One podcast episode generates a launch post, an episode highlight, and a newsletter mention. Each guest brings their own network. The series compounds over time. What's working on LinkedIn in 2025
📄
Carousels are the #1 format — and neither brand is using them
Document carousels generate +39% reach and +30% engagement vs. platform average. Avg carousel engagement: 24.42% vs. 4% for text+image. Neither Guidehouse nor Energetics uses this consistently. Biggest untapped opportunity for both.
🧑‍💼
Personal profiles reach 2–4× further than company pages
LinkedIn's 2025 algorithm prioritizes person-to-person content. Employee shares get 2× higher CTR. Guidehouse offsets this with showcase pages and executive profile reshares. The key move: senior team members posting original content from personal accounts and the company amplifying from its page.
🔗
Links in post bodies suppress organic reach — move them to comments
LinkedIn penalizes posts that send users off-platform. Both brands embed links in post bodies — a consistent mistake. Post the content, then add the link as the first comment immediately after publishing. This simple change improves distribution without changing any content.
⚡
Consistency compounds — 3–5× per week produces far better results than monthly bursts
Daily posting grows followers 8× faster than weekly. Energetics posts ~4×/month. Even reaching 3×/week would be a 3× improvement in algorithmic momentum and follower growth velocity.
📊
Polls drive impressions — not engagement, but reach
Polls achieve 1.78× reach vs. average but only 0.37× engagement. Use quarterly as an impression play — timely question, follow-up results post. Creates a 2-post cycle from 1 question. Neither brand currently uses polls.

The big picture
LinkedIn marketing has fundamentally shifted. AI made content production cheap — which made authentic, human, intentional content more valuable than ever. More posts are published daily than at any point in history. Fewer are being remembered.
How LinkedIn content has evolved
The shift — then vs. now
2019–2022 · The broadcast era
Post → broadcast. Companies posted announcements. Followers received. Likes were the goal.
Polished = credible. Professionally designed graphics, carefully worded corporate copy. Raw felt amateur.
Frequency was optional. 2–4 posts/month was acceptable. Audiences weren't saturated.
Success = likes and followers. Vanity metrics ruled every report.
→
2023–2025 · The authenticity era
Post → conversation. Algorithm rewards comments, saves, and shares. Content needs to make people stop and respond.
Real = credible. A genuine caption on a grainy event photo outperforms a polished infographic. Audiences can feel AI-generated content and scroll past it.
Frequency is the floor — intentionality is the ceiling. 3–5×/week builds momentum. Generic content just adds noise.
Success = impressions, saves, shares, and inbound inquiries. Likes say people saw it. Shares say people believed in it.
The AI content paradox
🤖
AI made production easy — which made authentic content rare and more valuable
The very thing AI enables is what makes human content stand out
AI tools can now draft a LinkedIn post in seconds. Thousands of companies are posting AI-polished, structurally perfect, generically insightful content daily. Audiences detect it by feel — not software. The posts that stop the scroll have specific names, real moments, genuine opinions. Those are things AI cannot fabricate convincingly.
📢
Content overload means intentionality is the only real differentiator
More posts published daily on LinkedIn than at any point in history
The answer to overload is not to post less — it's to post more specifically. A post for "utility program managers evaluating EV fleet adoption" outperforms one for "clean energy professionals" every time, even if the second audience is 10× larger. Narrow, specific content reaches the right people and earns shares into their networks.
✅
Use AI as a production tool — not a content creator
AI for speed, humans for voice
Use AI to accelerate production — draft structure, repurpose content, generate caption variations. Always add the human layer: the specific anecdote, the real name, the genuine take. AI helps maintain frequency. Human voice is what makes people trust you enough to share.
⚠️
AI tells to avoid — generic openers signal machine-written content immediately
The scroll-past signals audiences have learned to recognize
"In today's rapidly evolving landscape..." or "We are excited to share..." are the clearest AI content signals and get the lowest engagement. Start with a specific fact, a real name, or a genuine take. "Last Tuesday our team drove from DC to Huntsville for a client with 48 hours to respond to a DOE request" beats any AI version of that.
Rethinking success metrics
For a consulting firm, likes are the least important metric
❌
Likes
Tells you someone saw it and didn't object. Low friction. Low signal. A colleague liking your post out of loyalty moves no business needle.
✓
Impressions & reach
Measures brand awareness. A decision-maker who sees your post 6 times over 3 months will think of you when a contract opens. Presence over time matters more than any single post.
✓✓
Shares & saves
The highest-value signal. A share puts your brand in front of the sharer's entire network. A save means someone found it useful enough to return to. These drive real word-of-mouth.
The consulting firm business impact chain: Impressions → brand familiarity → trust → profile visits → inbound inquiry → RFP invitation → contract. A like is at step one. The goal of LinkedIn is to stay present in the minds of federal program officers, utility executives, and state energy directors long enough that when a contract opens, you're the first call.
Strategic north star
LinkedIn is not a likes machine — it is a brand awareness and trust-building engine. Shares and impressions matter more than likes. Intentionality matters more than volume. Know who you're talking to before you post.

Internal vs. external engagement — the blind spot
🔍
High likes don't always mean real reach — ask who is actually liking
The most important audit your marketing team isn't doing
When a post gets 50 likes, that number feels like success. But if 40 of those 50 are current employees liking out of team loyalty, the post only reached 10 people outside the company. Zero new brand impressions. Zero potential leads. The number looked good in a report but did nothing for the business. Energetics' comment data shows ~85% of comments are brief congratulations — a clear signal that most engagement is internal warmth, not external interest.
⚠ Signs engagement is mostly internal
→ Most likers have the same company in their profile
→ Comments are almost all "Congrats!" from teammates
→ Internal event posts outperform thought leadership
→ No unfamiliar names in the like list
→ Reposts are rare — content doesn't travel
✓ Signs engagement is genuinely external
→ Likers are from different companies in your industry
→ Comments add perspective or ask real questions
→ Reposts from people outside the organization
→ New followers spike after a post
→ Profile visits increase — decision-makers checking you out

A post written for federal program officers evaluating EV fleet deployment outperforms one for "clean energy professionals" every time. When the right person reads it, thinks "this is exactly my problem," and shares it with three peers — that's when LinkedIn becomes a real lead generation tool. A like from a colleague moves no business needle. A share from a utility VP puts the brand in front of their entire network. 🔄
Frequency builds familiarity — familiarity builds trust — trust wins contracts
Decision-makers need 6–8 exposures before they act. LinkedIn is where that happens.
No single post wins a contract. What wins is consistent presence in the feeds of decision-makers over months. Guidehouse posts ~17×/month. Energetics ~4×/month. The acquired firm should treat 3×/week as the minimum viable cadence. Use AI to maintain frequency — but always make each post feel human.

Hashtag strategy — 2–3 specific tags maximum, never decorative
On LinkedIn, hashtags are topic signals, not discovery engines. Less is more.
LinkedIn's algorithm has deprioritized hashtag-based reach since 2023. What hashtags do is categorize your post for topic-based surfacing. 2–3 specific hashtags signal genuine expertise. A wall of hashtags signals a marketing checklist — and undercuts authenticity.

✗ Avoid — too broad

Build owned media — a recurring content engine, not one-off posts
Guidehouse's podcast generated 3 top-performing posts from 1 series. Each episode = another post.

03
Make CSR a recurring brand pillar — document every community moment
Guidehouse posts CSR every 2–3 weeks. Avg 103 likes with genuine external comments.
CSR content is one of the only formats that consistently attracts people who don't already follow you. Document every volunteer day, every community initiative, every employee-led cause. Post within the same week it happens — immediacy matters.

04
Post 3× per week — frequency is the biggest structural gap
Guidehouse: 199 posts in 12 months (~17/mo). Energetics: 46 (~4/mo). 3×/week is the floor.
Daily posting grows followers 8× faster than weekly. Monday = people/culture. Wednesday = thought leadership or carousel. Friday = partner, event, or CSR. Links always go in the first comment — never the post body.
